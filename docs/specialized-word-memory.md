# Specialized-word memory

Repeated lower-case institutions, objects and practices can be translation terms
without being proper names. English discovery reserves at most four slots in the
existing sixteen-candidate wave budget. Possessives and short adjective sequences
are supported; ordinary words still require semantic classification and are not
automatically promoted by repetition.

The existing lexical call supplies a preferred target, meaning, semantic usage
scope and optional short forms. Source quotes are attached by the host. Model
self-ratings are neither requested nor used for admission, reuse or ranking.
A source-grounded sense can produce a `lexical_preference:<sense-id>` record
without becoming an independently corroborated concept. This is always `preferred` or
`contextual`, never a model-created hard lock. Manual/imported rendering rules keep
their precedence.

Preferences live in the existing SQLite knowledge journal and snapshots. Distinct
senses have separate records; their translator projection lists the meanings and
allowed surfaces in one contextual entry, avoiding competing rendering rules for
the same spelling. Only relevant terms enter each translation request. Established
preferences and their source evidence survive restart; a matching sense does not
need another lexical decision. A different sense remains free to use different
wording. This does not constrain sentence rhythm, imagery or character voice.

Source-grounded preferences can survive a deferred semantic translation issue.
The storage boundary verifies their soft policy and quotes against the immutable
source or its layout-free projection. Other memory and style observations from a
quality-deferred window remain isolated.

`book audit` and standard-delivery quality reports enumerate every exact source
occurrence, including block, paragraph, source offsets and aligned target text.
`preferred_surface_present` means only that an allowed surface appears in the
corresponding paragraph. `variant_or_missing` is a review clue, not a verdict:
another sense, a valid paraphrase or an omission may explain it. `untranslated`
and `unaligned` distinguish missing text from an unpaired paragraph. No global
replacement or extra model pass is launched by this scan, and its soft findings
do not block delivery of structurally valid complete text.

Host-discovered recurrent nouns retain occurrence receipts even when classified
as ordinary words. These observations can carry an attested default into later
windows; differing attested senses do not become one universal rendering.
Chapter review can compare bounded aligned examples of legacy ordinary-word
decisions across the source. These read-only hints do not create term locks or
authorize edits outside the current review scope.

Historical score fields remain readable for audit compatibility, but are omitted
from model-facing projections. Existing revision IDs, journals and earlier exports
are not rewritten. Legacy non-null SQL score columns contain inert zero padding for
new rows; no semantic decision reads those columns. Source/target evidence, sense,
contextual policy and scope remain the admission criteria. Corrections to translated
text use grounded bounded repair or scoped terminology and audited retrofit.
